The soc.sucks manifesto

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT" and "SHOULD" are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119. (as old as your SIEM)

  1. Every alert MUST be worth a human's attention.An alert nobody reads is a lie.
  2. Every detection MUST be tested before it ships.Untested detections are guesses. Guesses in production are liabilities.
  3. Coverage MUST be measured against your actual risk.A green heatmap on a slide protects the slide.
  4. Noise MUST go down over time, not up.Volume is not vigilance. An inbox is not a defense.
  5. Fix what creates the alerts, not how fast you close them.Auto-closing garbage faster is still garbage.
  6. Severity MUST come from what the business stands to lose.The vendor default doesn't know what pays your salary.
  7. "Critical" MUST be rare.When everything is critical, nothing is.
  8. Detections MUST live in version control."The old version is in my Downloads folder" is not version control.
  9. Detection logic MUST outlive your SIEM contract.Rewriting 600 rules by hand is not a migration plan. It's a hostage situation.
  10. A rule that goes silent MUST page someone.Otherwise your SIEM is a diary with a license fee.
  11. Every rule MUST have an owner, a test, and a reason to exist.Delete the rest. Yes, that one too.
  12. Threat intel MUST become detections in days, not quarters.A technique that sits in a PDF for a month is history, not intelligence.
  13. Burnout MUST be treated as a signal-quality problem.You can't hire your way out of 47,000 false positives.
  14. Tier 1 MUST NOT be a life sentence.Closing duplicates for two years is not a career path. It's a symptom.
  15. Juniors MUST be able to ship safely.They don't need permission to matter. They need guardrails.
  16. Analysts MUST hunt.You were hired to hunt threats, not click around like a monkey.

This needs to change.
